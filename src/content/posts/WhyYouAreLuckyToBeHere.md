Singaporeans are obsessed with lucky draws. When a vehicle breaks down, not only the lanes in the same direction slows down because of the broken down vehicle but the lanes from the other direction as well - because people think they will strike 4D from the from license plate numbers.

When a queue forms in front of a nondescript shop within the HDB heartlands, its almost guaranteed to be a Singapore Pools shop.

But maybe you are one of the lucky few who have struck lottery and are now reading this from (insert idyllic island/ resort here), being attended by attractive women or men waiting on your every command?

But what if I say everyone reading this is lucky. In fact, very lucky. Extremely lucky. As- tound- ing- ly lucky. To be here.

Consider this. The chances of someone being born right now is the result of each parent meeting each other, both taking fancy, agreeing to be exclusive, rejecting the advances of other suiters, accepting each other's flaws and shortcomings. making sure both are aligned across long term goals and values, lasting long enough to copulate, overcoming life's uncertainties and bear an offspring.

Not so easy.

**Medical complications**

[Sperm count has been dropping globally](https://www.forbes.com/sites/neilhowe/2017/10/02/youre-not-the-man-your-father-was/?sh=2f4a5f238b7f), so what else is new? The culprit? Probably diet and microplastics.

Diet is nothing new - with [obesity rates raising sharply globally](https://time.com/6264865/global-obesity-rates-increasing/) and a [clear link](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3955331/) between obesity and testosterone (*overweight is just anything more than 25 bmi, folks. Obese is 30*), its no wonder why our soldiers are not enough/malformed.

[A recent research from China](https://particleandfibretoxicology.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12989-022-00453-2) has shown that plastics can be harmful to us because of just how ubiquitous it is.

Remember the food web in our primary school science text book? Microplastics thrown by humans are consumed by small fishes which are then eaten by bigger fishes and then eaten by us. Yeah, you know how it goes.

*All your plastics are belong to us*

**Biological Process**

When I was hunting for a job, I attended many *many* interviews and the interview is all about selecting the right candidate for the job like screening for personality fit, accessing your technical knowledge to maybe I don't know, presentation skills.

The same can almost be said of the fertilised egg's journey to a fetus.

We all know about the sperm's journey from the landing site to the target site: the woman's white blood cells would attack the sperm, the sperms may not be able to propel itself properly, there may be insufficient sperms, etc ,etc. But what other perils lie beyond our single-celled hero?

The first screening of the embryo is whether it is able to latch on onto the endometrium (which is the layer of tissue that lines the uterus). In the book ["This is your brain on birth control"](https://www.amazon.com/This-Your-Brain-Birth-Control-ebook/dp/B07MYLLP21), Susan Hill explains that rather than *improve* the chances of implantation, it hampers it. How do they know that? Read on.

> *How do we know that? Well, scientists have tried to implant mouse embryos in a bunch of other locations on the body, expecting them to wither away and die since they were deprived of the endometrial lining to nourish and sustain them. Much to their surprise, what they found was exactly the opposite. **Not only did these embryos not die; they flourished**. These little despots mercilessly burrowed their way through the tissues wherever they were implanted, destroying everything in their wake as they rummaged around for arteries to tap into to fuel their continued growth and expansion. The endometrium is actually one of the hardest places to grow an embryo, since the environment is downright inhospitable to a newly fertilized egg.*
>
> — Susan Hill on how they found out the endometrium was hazardous to the implantation process from her book "This is your brain on birth control"

Susan theorises that from an evolutionary point of view, this will allow the mother's body to "test" the embryo if it is "fit" enough as a proxy to survivability in the wild. Otherwise the failed embryo will simply be expunged from the body after the cycle ends. Thus ensuring that the fertilised egg is of some quality if it is able to latch onto the wall.

She goes on to mention that 32% of the embryos meet similar fate.

<!-- IMAGE: ian-dooley-d1upkifd04a-unsplash.jpg -->
*Well, I know as men, our failure rate is wayyyyyy higher than that*

Often in movies we see the first sign of indication of pregnancy is the woman just hurling her guts out. And that my friends is the next screening process.

You see, assuming the embryo passes the implanting test, it needs to be able to produce a hormone enough to stop the menstruation cycle lest it gets expunged as well.

This hormone is called Human Chorionic Gonadotropin or [hCG](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/22489-human-chorionic-gonadotropin) for short and performs a host of functions including stopping the menstruation cycle, thickening the uterus lining and causing morning sickness and some food aversion that may contain teratogens which are compounds that is thought to be bad for fetal development.

Coincidentally testing for the presence of hCG is how pregnancy kits work as well.

An additional 24% of fertilised eggs do not produce enough hCG to stop the menstruation cycle and gets flushed out of the body as well.

## Wrapping Up

Do you think you are lucky now? The odds of our parents finding each other and avoiding life's mishaps and misfortunes to ultimately end up in each others arms then doing the horizontal (or vertical maybe) dance to have you as a gem in their lives.

If anything, if you feel lucky, go and try your luck at 4D.
